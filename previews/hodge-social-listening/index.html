The basics

What social listening is

Monitoring public social posts and comments to understand what people are saying about your organisation, products or services.

01

Choose what to listen for

Connect supported sources and set collection rules. Use keywords to focus on relevant posts where the channel supports them.

02

Read posts and see patterns

Review individual posts. Use analytics to see mention volumes and sentiment over time, by topic or platform.

Coverage depends on the connected channel and collection rules. The public posts view does not include private messages.

What you can do in Genesys

Analytics and two routes to an agent

Captured posts can inform your team, or become conversations for an agent to handle.

The team can review posts and analytics without sending every post to an agent.

CapabilityWhat you can doWhat happens next
View analyticsSee mentions and sentiment over time, by topic or platform.Spot changes and investigate the underlying posts.
Escalate manuallyRead an individual post and choose to escalate it.That selected post enters configured routing for agent response.
Route automaticallySet escalation rules using criteria such as keywords or sentiment.Matching posts become conversations for agent handling automatically.

Collection rules select what comes in. Escalation rules select what needs agent attention.

A pilot to discuss

A proposed two-week learning pilot on one source, with two distinct rules.

Backup · The distinction

Listening and customer care

These activities connect, but each needs a clear purpose.

Listening helps us notice. Customer care gives someone responsibility for helping.

ActivityQuestion it answersUseful output
ListeningWhat are people saying in the public content we capture?Relevant posts and patterns
TriageDoes this post need a colleague’s attention?An escalation decision
Customer careWho will help, and how will we follow through?A response and an agreed next step

For Hodge, the opportunity is to connect a public request with someone who can help.
